## How she talks

- **Opens with "All right," "All right, cool," or "So,"** then straight to the point. Never "Hey guys" or "In this video".
- **"Okay?" as a check-in** after the line she wants to land: "Follow the rules, do things by the book, and don't get yourself in trouble, okay?"
- **Asks the question, then answers it.** "Property's abandoned, what do you do?" "Keys are back. Done? Not yet."
- **Says it three ways for emphasis.** "Tenant just leaves. They're not communicating. They just leave." "Take pictures. A lot of pictures. Too many pictures."
- **Acts out what people say,** as a quick quote: "something to the effect of, 'We're pretty sure you abandoned the property. If not, let us know.'" "Hey, we've done everything in good faith."
- **Candid and a little dry.** "I don't know how we're supposed to figure that out, but you do." "So, not great." "Doesn't happen all that often, to be honest." "Honestly, it's kind of gross."
- **Steps as "you're going to" or a short imperative.** "You're going to wait 5 days before taking any further action." "If it doesn't work, fix it." "Stick with the basic."
- **Law in plain words, never citations.** "Washington law requires...", "there's very strict regulations". No RCW numbers out loud, no legal advice; "by the book" and "don't get yourself in trouble" carry it. Pair the rule with the human reason when there is one (Brittany, 2026-11-18, on belongings left behind: "it's WA regulation, it's also... good manners"): "Washington's strict on this, and honestly, it's just good manners. It's their stuff."
- **Lands on the owner's interest.** "It ends up costing you less." "Protects both you and your investment" is the blog's phrasing, not hers; she says "so you don't get yourself in trouble down the line."
- **"Basically", "just", "like", "pretty much"** as connective tissue, sparingly. "Like, actually clean."
- **Talks to one owner as "you"; Sagareus is "we".** "We, by default, always recommend..." "In our experience..."
- **Names things plainly.** "Certified mail", "deadbolt", "smoke alarms", "trash out", not "remediation services".
- **Closes with a bottom line.** "The bottom line is..." For the before/after templates the bottom line is always the turnover standard: "Safe, clean, working, warm." then "Rent ready."
- Pace is fast, about 3.3 words a second. Before/after videos are 30 s or less including the 3 s end card (Brittany, 2026-11-18), so the script is about 60 to 65 words; cut lines, never speed up the voice.

## Script rules

- Write it to be **read aloud by staff in her register**: contractions, short sentences, one idea per line, a beat between lines. Never generate the voice (no AI voiceovers, CLAUDE.md).
- **Every step comes from the linked SOP.** Don't add steps, deadlines or legal claims the SOP doesn't have; leave out SOP details that don't fit the footage.
- **The linked SOP is the compliance source** (Brittany, 2026-11-18): staff must paste the SOP link (Junk, Trashed); every step and claim comes from it or from the footage, and Brittany's final approval is the check. Reno has no SOP; instead staff must write 1 to 2 lines on the card about what is happening in the video (for example "Repair + Upgrade: replaced water damaged subfloor, upgraded carpet to LVP"), and every claim comes only from that note or the footage. If the card has no note, Claude asks for it in the chat and adds the answer to the card; no note, no Reno video. Same for a missing SOP link: Claude asks for it; no SOP link, no Junk or Trashed video. The plugin carries no positions file. Nothing about deposits, charges or deductions (Nov 6 rule).
- Her bluntness never lands on residents: joke about the bags, the carpet, the oven, never about the people.
- Say **"resident"** in scripts (Sagareus naming rule), even though she says "tenant" on camera.
- No investor jargon, no fee numbers, no screening thresholds, no addresses or staff names, no em dashes in anything on screen or in captions.
- In RECORDING.md, mark lines that are nearly hers verbatim with ★.
